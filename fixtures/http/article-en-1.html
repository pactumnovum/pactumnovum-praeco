Richest 1% gained more than the bottom half in Indonesia and Brazil, study finds

A study published on Monday compares how wealth and income are shared between the richest and the poorest households in two of the largest economies in Asia and Latin America.

The richest 1% of adults in Indonesia held 31.4% of household wealth in 2025, according to the study. The poorest half of adults held 4.7%.

In Brazil, the top 10% received 58.6% of national income, while the bottom 50% received 9.8%.

The combined fortunes of the country’s billionaires reached US$214 billion, up from US$171 billion a year earlier.

Researchers said the share of the richest 1% could reach 40% by 2030 if current trends continue.

The authors used household surveys, tax records and published rich lists, and they describe the limits of each source in an annex. They call for better data on top wealth in both countries, and for regular publication of the figures so that changes can be followed over time.
